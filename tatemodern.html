Impressionist Artworks at Tate Modern, London

The Tate Modern in London primarily focuses on modern and contemporary art from 1900 onward, so its collection of Impressionist artworks is limited compared to Tate Britain (which houses most pre-1900 British and international works, including many Impressionist pieces) or the National Gallery. Core Impressionism (roughly 1860s–1890s) is underrepresented, but the museum features late works by Impressionist artists like Claude Monet, whose career extended into the 20th century, as well as Post-Impressionist artists like Pierre Bonnard and Henri Matisse, whose styles evolved from Impressionist influences. These works are part of the free permanent collection displays, which are thematically organized rather than strictly chronological. Displays rotate periodically, so not all works are always on view, and locations can change. All listed artworks are in the Natalie Bell Building (the original main structure on Bankside), accessible via the Turbine Hall entrance.

Below is a list of key Impressionist and Post-Impressionist artists with artworks at Tate Modern, grouped by display theme and floor for clarity. Locations are based on standard setups as of recent confirmations (subject to change; check the Tate app or website for real-time info). URLs link to the official Tate pages, which include high-resolution photos and zoomable views.

Level 2: In the Studio (Themed around artists' processes and domestic scenes)

This display explores how artists capture everyday life and creative spaces, often with vibrant color use echoing Post-Impressionism.

  • Pierre Bonnard (Post-Impressionist, associated with the Nabis group influenced by Impressionism): The Bowl of Milk (c. 1919)

Level 2: Artist and Society (Themed around social themes and bold forms)

This area highlights innovative uses of color and shape in modern contexts.

  • Henri Matisse (Post-Impressionist/Fauvist, building on Impressionist color theories): The Snail (1953)

Level 4: Materials and Objects (Themed around materiality and abstraction)

This display connects late Impressionist works to abstract art, often pairing them with modern pieces like those by Mark Rothko for contrast.

  • Claude Monet (Impressionist): Water-Lilies (after 1916)
    This large-scale panel is displayed in a dedicated room emphasizing its immersive qualities, sometimes referred to as the Monet/Rothko room due to thematic links.

Additional Post-Impressionist-influenced works may rotate in, but the above are consistently highlighted. For a full, up-to-date search of the Tate collection (including filters for "on display" and "Impressionism"), visit https://www.tate.org.uk/search?type=artwork&q=impressionism. Photos on the artwork pages are free to view; high-resolution downloads or prints are available via Tate's shop with permissions.
